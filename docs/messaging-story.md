## 1. Who she is (one person, two doors)

**Meet Megan.** She's 32 and lives in Kentucky, with a 5-year-old, a 2-year-old, and maybe a third on her mind. Family and health matter deeply to her, and she researches everything.

**A normal day for her:**
- She needs a second coffee to make it to school pickup, and still hits a wall around 3 p.m.
- She's exhausted at bedtime, then wide awake at 2 a.m. with her mind racing.
- There's more hair in the shower drain than there used to be. She gets headaches, has cold hands and feet, and craves sugar in the afternoon.
- She snaps at her kids over spilled milk, then sits in the car for an extra minute feeling guilty.
- Her doctor checked her out and said it's "just part of having little kids."

**Her son, 5:**
- He eats the same five beige foods, and dinner is a standoff.
- He takes an hour to fall asleep, then ends up in her bed at 3 a.m.
- He has tummy aches, goes days without pooping, and has dry, itchy patches on his elbows.
- He melts down after school over his socks feeling wrong.

**What she's tried:** She's saved a dozen Instagram videos about magnesium. She spent $70 on a kids' supplement bundle she isn't sure about. She asked her mom group and got five different answers. She ends up Googling "why is my kid always constipated" at 11 p.m.

**She doesn't need more information. She needs someone to look at *her* family and tell her what to do first.**

- **Door 1 (For You):** Megan herself: getting ready for pregnancy, recovering after birth, or in the busy mom years.
- **Door 2 (For Your Child):** Megan's kid, ages 4–17. Megan is still the buyer.

### Two versions of the same mom (write to both)
**A. "I just thought this was motherhood."** She hasn't been to the doctor about it, and may not realize anything is off. So **don't name symptoms; describe how they show up in her life:**
- Trouble concentrating → *rereads the same email three times and still loses her train of thought*
- Fatigue → *counting down to bedtime by 2 p.m.*
- Poor sleep → *exhausted, then can't fall asleep once she gets there*
- Brain fog → *walks into a room and forgets why she's there*
- Irritability → *little things set her off, and the guilt follows her all day*

**B. "I know something's off, but I'm stuck."** It's been this way so long it feels normal. Her checkups say she's fine enough that no one digs deeper. She's done the "right" things (eaten better, taken vitamins) and nothing sticks. And she has no time or energy left to figure it out for herself.

### Alicia's story (the proof)
Alicia was exhausted and pushing through too. **Specific testing made her own plan clear.** With balanced minerals, whole-food nutrition focused on what her body actually needed (no fads, no keto), and a few targeted supplements, she has **more energy now, postpartum with two kids, than she did in her first pregnancy.** Every body is different, which is why she starts with testing.
> Always tell it as *her* experience. Never promise the same result to the reader.

## 3. Why she's looking for help *now*
She's tired of trial and error. She wants a **specific, simple plan for her family**, not another generic list, from someone she trusts.

## 4. How Alicia helps (the guide)
- **Empathy:** She's a mom too, and has had her own seasons of exhaustion, hormone, and sleep struggles.
- **Authority:** Family nurse practitioner, HTMA practitioner, certified in brain health.
- **Her promise (from her own homework):** make wellness simpler, so mom feels better and the whole family benefits.

## 5. Why "start with the basics" matters (plain-language version of "foundations first")
Before adding more supplements or complicated protocols, get the basics right: the minerals, food, sleep, and daily rhythms the body runs on. When those are low, everything else is harder. When they're right, other changes work better. It's simpler, often cheaper, and a smarter place to start.

> **Website rule:** say it like this ("start with the basics your body runs on"). Avoid jargon like "foundations-first approach," "HTMA-led path," or "interpreted in context."

### "Root cause," Alicia's way (OK to use)
To Alicia, "root" means the **foundations**: the minerals and nutrients every cell needs to function, which then build up into how you feel and live every day (energy, mood, sleep, focus, how kids grow). Say it that way: *"we start at the root, with the foundations everything else is built on."*
> **Don't** claim the *test* "finds the root cause" or "proves" anything. The root is the philosophy (start with the foundations), not a promise about what HTMA shows.

## 6. Why HTMA is the front door (but not the whole house)
- It's an **easy yes**: an at-home hair sample with no needles, a flat price, and real results about her (or her child's) minerals.
- It turns "what should I try?" into **a plan based on *her* results**.
- After that, Alicia brings everything else: food, habits, sleep, stress, and her home.

## 7. The 3-step plan (repeat everywhere)
1. **Test:** order your at-home kit and mail in a small hair sample.
2. **Review:** walk through your results with Alicia in plain language.
3. **Plan:** get a simple, step-by-step plan for food, minerals, and daily habits, plus a follow-up.

## 8. What success looks like (always "may")
More steady energy, better sleep, more patience. A kid who may eat and sleep a little easier. A calmer home. And the confidence that you're doing the right things, in the right order.

## 9. What's at stake if nothing changes
More money on supplements that might not fit, more 2 a.m. wake-ups and dinner standoffs, and more days of holding everyone else together while she feels worse.

## 10. One-liner (use as the backbone)
> **"Feel like yourself again, and help your kids feel their best, with a simple, personal plan that starts with an easy at-home mineral test."**
