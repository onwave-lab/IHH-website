## Quick answer

HTMA and blood tests answer different questions, so the "better" choice depends on what you're trying to learn. **Blood work shows what's happening right now** — your circulating levels of vitamins, hormones, blood sugar, and inflammation. **HTMA shows what your body has stored and used over the last 3 to 4 months** — the mineral patterns blood work doesn't capture. For most women preparing for pregnancy or recovering postpartum, the most useful first step is a functional review of any recent blood work, paired with an HTMA panel — not one or the other.

## Why this question keeps coming up

I work with women who have been told their labs look "normal" for years, and yet they still feel exhausted, foggy, or off. Blood work is a powerful tool, but it was designed primarily to flag disease, not to optimize health. That's why a result inside the reference range can still mask a functional imbalance.

HTMA is a different lens. It doesn't replace blood work — it shows you something blood work isn't built to show.

## How they compare

| What it measures | Standard Blood Test | HTMA (Hair Tissue Mineral Analysis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Window of time** | A snapshot — what's in circulation right now | A 3 to 4 month history of cellular mineral activity |
| **What it captures** | Hormones, blood sugar, lipids, inflammation, some minerals (mostly serum levels) | Minerals and heavy metals stored in tissue, ratios between key minerals, stress and thyroid patterns |
| **Key minerals shown** | Some (e.g., serum magnesium, ferritin, sodium) — often in narrow ranges | Magnesium, zinc, sodium, potassium, copper, calcium, manganese, plus toxic metals |
| **Reflects long-term status?** | No — fluctuates daily | Yes — reflects what cells have been doing for months |
| **Collection** | Blood draw (lab visit, fasting often required) | At-home hair sample sent to lab |
| **What it's best at** | Diagnosing or ruling out disease, tracking acute treatment | Spotting depletion patterns, stress response, mineral imbalances before they become disease |
| **What it can miss** | Cellular-level mineral depletion, long-term oxidative or stress patterns | Acute infection, current hormone levels, real-time blood sugar |
| **Insurance covered?** | Usually | No — out-of-pocket, but at-home and one-time |
| **Best use case** | Disease screening, acute symptom workup | Pregnancy prep, postpartum recovery, "labs are normal but I feel off" |

## When blood work is the right first step

If you have symptoms that point toward disease — unexplained weight loss, severe fatigue, persistent pain, signs of thyroid disease, or you haven't had a physical in years — start with blood work. A standard panel from your primary care provider, plus a functional read of those results, will tell you whether something acute needs attention.

If you have recent blood work that came back "normal" but you don't feel well, the next step isn't necessarily more blood work — it's a [functional lab review](/services/lab-review.html) of what you already have.

## When HTMA is the right first step

HTMA tends to be the more useful starting point when:

- Your labs have been called "normal" but you feel exhausted, foggy, or off
- You're preparing for pregnancy and want to understand your mineral reserves before conception
- You're recovering postpartum and trying to rebuild what depleted during pregnancy and birth
- You suspect long-term stress, blood sugar swings, or burnout patterns
- You want to spot mineral depletion before it shows up in blood work or symptoms

This is the lens I use most often with the women in my practice, and it's the foundation of the [Intentional Health Reset](/services/intentional-reset.html).

## When you need both

For most women preparing for pregnancy, recovering postpartum, or working through "labs are normal but I feel off," the highest-leverage approach is both — recent blood work read functionally, paired with an HTMA panel. The two together show:

- What's circulating right now (blood) AND what cells have been doing for months (HTMA)
- Whether a "low-normal" iron, thyroid, or vitamin D matches a tissue-level pattern
- Whether stress and mineral depletion are driving symptoms blood work can't see

## A note on accuracy and limitations

HTMA is a screening and pattern-recognition tool, not a diagnostic test for disease. It is best used to inform lifestyle, nutrition, and supplemental support — not to diagnose or treat medical conditions. As with any test, results should be interpreted by a practitioner who understands both the methodology and your full health picture.

This is why I don't sell HTMA panels without the practitioner review. The number on the report matters less than the pattern it shows, and the pattern only matters if it gets translated into action.
