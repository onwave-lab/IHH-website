# Keyword Research Checklist — Intention Holistic Health

*A do-it-by-hand checklist for Alicia or Kyle. No code required. Free tools only unless noted.*

---

## Before you start: three things specific to IHH

**1. Health content is held to a higher bar.** Google classifies health and wellness as "Your Money or Your Life" (YMYL) content and judges it on Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust. This is an advantage, not an obstacle: Alicia is a nurse practitioner. Surface her credentials on every page the keywords lead to. Generic wellness blogs can't compete with a licensed clinician on trust.

**2. "Competition: High" in Google's tool does NOT mean hard to rank.** Google Keyword Planner's "Competition" column measures how many *advertisers* bid on a term, not how hard it is to show up in normal search results. Don't let a "High" label scare you off a relevant keyword.

**3. You have two completely different audiences. Keep two separate lists.**
- **B2C / patient-facing:** moms, women with hormone concerns, people searching symptoms and "nontoxic swaps." They search informally and emotionally.
- **B2B / partner-facing:** clinics and birth workers in Kentucky who might refer to or contract Alicia as an outsourced nutrition specialist. They search professionally ("nutrition referral partner," "prenatal nutrition support for clinics").

Run the whole checklist *twice* — once per audience. Mixing them produces a muddy list that serves neither.

## The checklist

### Step 1 — Write down your seed terms
- [ ] List 5–10 plain-English phrases describing what IHH does, for the audience you're working on right now.
  - B2C examples: `hormone balancing nutrition`, `nontoxic swaps for hormones`, `prenatal nutrition`, `holistic nutrition for women`.
  - B2B examples: `outsourced nutrition specialist`, `nutrition support for birth workers`, `clinic nutrition referral`.
- [ ] Add your location where it fits: `prenatal nutritionist Kentucky`, `holistic nutrition Louisville`.

### Step 2 — Expand with autocomplete (free, 10 minutes)
- [ ] Type each seed into the Google search bar and write down every suggestion that drops down.
- [ ] Do the same in the YouTube search bar (people phrase health questions differently when looking for video).
- [ ] Add a letter after the seed to force more: `hormone nutrition a`, `hormone nutrition b`, etc. Each letter reveals new long-tail phrases.
- [ ] Look at the "People also ask" box and the "Related searches" at the bottom of the Google results page. Copy anything relevant.

### Step 3 — Get the numbers from Google Keyword Planner (free)
- [ ] Sign in to a Google Ads account (you can create one without running ads).
- [ ] Open **Tools → Keyword Planner → Discover new keywords**.
- [ ] Paste your seed terms. Set the location to your target (Kentucky, or your service radius).
- [ ] Read these columns for each keyword:
  - **Avg. monthly searches** — is there enough demand? (treat the numbers as rough ranges, not exact)
  - **Competition** — ignore for ranking purposes; only matters if you ever run ads
  - **Top of page bid** — a high bid is a clue that the keyword leads to money/clients
- [ ] Export the list or copy the keywords with decent volume into a spreadsheet.

### Step 4 — Check the trend (free, optional)
- [ ] Drop your top few keywords into Google Trends.
- [ ] Note anything **seasonal** (e.g. "detox" spikes in January) and anything **rising vs declining**. Favor flat-or-rising terms.

### Step 5 — Judge each keyword on four questions
For every keyword that survived, mark:
- [ ] **Relevant?** Does it actually match what IHH offers? (Cut the rest, no matter the volume.)
- [ ] **Intent match?** What does the searcher want — to *learn* (informational), to *choose a provider* (commercial), or to *book* (transactional)? Label each one.
- [ ] **Can Alicia answer it with authority?** If yes, it plays to the credential advantage. Flag these as priority.
- [ ] **Beatable?** Search the keyword and look at who ranks. If it's all WebMD/Healthline, pick a more specific long-tail version. If it's other small clinics and blogs, you can compete.

### Step 6 — Group into clusters
- [ ] Sort the surviving keywords into themes (e.g. "hormone-friendly food swaps," "prenatal nutrition basics," "endocrine disruptors in the home").
- [ ] Each cluster = one piece of content (article, video, or lead magnet). One primary keyword in the title, 2–3 secondary keywords woven into the body.

### Step 7 — Map clusters to the funnel
- [ ] For **B2C** clusters: most will be informational (top-of-funnel blog/video that builds trust and captures email).
- [ ] For **B2B** clusters: these are commercial — they belong on service/partnership pages and in outreach, not blog posts.
- [ ] Note which clusters could become a **lead magnet** (e.g. "Nontoxic Swaps Checklist for Hormone Health").

### Step 8 — Hand off to content
- [ ] When a cluster becomes content, apply Alicia's voice rules: no em dashes; no back-to-back short sentences used as a listing device; never address readers as people who are "already doing everything right" or optimizing.
- [ ] Keep claims informational and educational. Avoid promising to treat, cure, or diagnose — that crosses into regulated medical-claim territory and undercuts trust.

## Quick-reference: free tool stack

| Job | Tool | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Long-tail expansion | Google + YouTube autocomplete | Free |
| Volume + bid data | Google Keyword Planner | Free (Google Ads account) |
| Trend + seasonality | Google Trends | Free |
| Question mining | "People also ask" + Reddit search | Free |
| Difficulty gut-check | Just Google it and read the front page | Free |

If IHH later wants real difficulty scores and bigger keyword sets, that's the point to add one paid tool (Keywords Everywhere is the cheapest entry; Ahrefs/Semrush if budget allows). Don't pay until the free workflow is a habit.
